Why your business emails go to spam

Sending an email and actually landing in the inbox are not the same thing. For a small business, that difference can mean missed quotes, ignored invoices, and follow-ups that never get seen.

The simple version

Email providers are trying to decide whether your message is trustworthy. They look at your domain, sending system, authentication records, reputation, and user behavior. If the signals are weak or inconsistent, the email may land in spam even when nothing looks “broken” on your end.

The three records people hear about

SPF says which services are allowed to send email for your domain. DKIM adds a signature that proves the message was not altered. DMARC tells receiving providers how to treat messages that fail those checks. The names sound technical, but the business point is simple: your domain needs to prove that your email is legitimate.

Why new business email can still struggle

A new domain has little or no sending reputation. Even with proper setup, blasting emails immediately or using inconsistent tools can look suspicious. New business email should be configured carefully and used normally before it is pushed hard for campaigns or outreach.

What a real check should include

A proper review does not stop at “the email sent.” It should check the domain records, provider alignment, test messages, Gmail and Outlook behavior, spam-folder placement, and whether replies go where they should.

The business risk

The danger is that you usually do not get a clean error message. The customer simply does not respond. That makes email deliverability feel like a sales problem when it may be a systems problem.
