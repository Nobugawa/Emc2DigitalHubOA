Plain-English digital guide

How not to get locked out of everything

Security is supposed to protect your business. Poorly planned security can also lock the owner out of the domain, website, email, payments, Google profile, or social accounts.

The hidden ownership problem

Many small businesses do not know who controls the domain, hosting, Google Workspace, website admin, Facebook page, Stripe account, or booking tool. Sometimes it is the owner. Sometimes it is a former employee, freelancer, web designer, spouse, or forgotten email address.

Security without continuity is fragile

Two-factor authentication is good. Passkeys can be good. Authenticator apps can be good. But if the only recovery method is a lost phone, old number, personal email, or former employee account, your security layer becomes a lockout risk.

What owners should document

Every business should know the registrar, host, email admin, website admin, payment processor, Google Business Profile owner, recovery email, recovery phone, backup-code storage method, and at least one trusted secondary admin where appropriate.

The mistake to avoid

Do not wait until a phone is lost, employee leaves, card expires, or developer disappears. Access continuity is easiest to fix before an emergency.

A practical first step

Create a business access map. It does not need to expose passwords. It should identify who owns what, how recovery works, and which accounts are critical to revenue and operations.
